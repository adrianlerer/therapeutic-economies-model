# Source and claim audit

## Provenance boundary

The manuscript distinguishes four layers:

| Layer | Material | Permitted claim |
|---|---|---|
| Primary cultural sources | Rieff (1966); Luri (2021) | Intellectual motivation and author-specific concepts |
| Economic and evolutionary antecedents | Kornai; Hannan and Freeman; Henrich; Pierson; Rodrik; World Bank | Mechanisms and rival interpretations stated by those sources |
| Authorial extension | therapeutic protection regime; protective selection inversion | Proposed theoretical constructs, not attributed to antecedents |
| Simulation | committed equations and synthetic outputs | Internal sufficiency only |

## Terminology audit

- `therapeutic economy` has prior use in medical anthropology, especially Nguyen (2005), with a different meaning concerning treatment options and access. No novelty claim is made.
- `therapeutic protection regime` is the precise label used for the political-economic mechanism.
- An exact-phrase web search for `protective selection inversion` was conducted on 13 September 2026 and returned no scholarly match. This is limited evidence, not proof of universal priority.

## Claim ceilings

- No equation is estimated or calibrated to Argentina.
- No scenario is a welfare analysis or policy forecast.
- The activity proxy is not GDP, productivity, employment, or social welfare.
- The model does not show that all protection produces dependence.
- The sensitivity grid is not a global robustness analysis.
- EPT terminology is a conditional mapping pending evidence of transmission and differential retention.
- The paper does not claim an evolutionary stable strategy.

## Rival hypotheses retained

Macroeconomic instability, infrastructure and finance constraints, strategic externalities, private market power, and explicit distributional insurance can explain persistence or low capability without the proposed mechanism.
